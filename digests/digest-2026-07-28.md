# Pharma Trend Digest — 2026-07-28

## ADC

### Signal Items

**B7-H3 ADC DS-7300a gains a ferroptosis combination angle in TP53-deficient tumors.** New preclinical work shows that inducing ferroptosis can boost the efficacy of DS-7300a (ifinatamab deruxtecan), Daiichi Sankyo's clinical-stage B7-H3-directed ADC, specifically in TP53-deficient tumor models (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42509416/). This matters because TP53 loss is common and often associated with chemo/ADC resistance — a rational combination strategy that restores sensitivity in this genetic subset could meaningfully extend the addressable population for an ADC that's already in active clinical development, and points to ferroptosis-inducers as a combo class worth watching across the ADC field more broadly.

**A new modality emerges: antibody-oligonucleotide-drug conjugates (AODC).** Researchers describe a high-payload AODC platform designed for targeted tumor chemo-immunotherapy, essentially merging ADC payload delivery with oligonucleotide (e.g., immune-stimulatory or gene-silencing) cargo on a single antibody scaffold (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501801/). This signals continued platform diversification beyond classical cytotoxin-payload ADCs — bifunctional conjugates that pair chemotherapy with immunomodulation in one molecule are an emerging design trend worth tracking as next-gen ADC architecture, alongside bispecific-ADCs and immune-stimulating ADCs (ISACs).

### Quick Hits

- A novel murine IgG-oxaliplatin (MIgG-OXA) ADC showed preclinical antitumor activity in lung adenocarcinoma models, an early-stage proof-of-concept for platinum-payload ADCs in solid tumors (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42500564/).

## Bispecifics

### Signal Items

- **A new VEGF-A/Ang-2 bispecific adds to the crowded anti-angiogenic pipeline.** Researchers report a rationally designed bispecific antibody that simultaneously neutralizes VEGF-A and Ang-2 to inhibit angiogenesis (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501945/). The dual-target angiogenesis approach (echoing faricimab's mechanism) continues to attract new entrants at the design/refolding stage — worth watching whether this moves beyond bench-scale expression work into functional or in vivo validation, but it signals that VEGF-A/Ang-2 remains a template other groups want to build variants against rather than a space Roche has locked up.

- **Analytical tooling is catching up to bispecific complexity.** A paper describes expanded biolayer interferometry (BLI) applications for detecting and quantifying residual biomolecules in bispecifics and AAV viral particle preparations, with improved accuracy, precision, and sensitivity (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42505466/). This matters less for headline drug news and more for the "boring but essential" side of the field: as bispecific formats proliferate, CMC/QC methods need to keep pace with impurity and residual-protein detection across increasingly heterogeneous molecules — this is a sign that analytical infrastructure is a live area of investment alongside molecule design itself.

## NAMs (New Approach Methodologies)

### Signal Items

- **Organ-on-chip moves toward real-time, continuous readouts.** A review on decoding cellular dynamics and microenvironmental responses in organ-on-chip systems via real-time sensing (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42508692/) points to where the field is heading: not just static endpoint organoid assays, but continuous sensing of cell behavior and microenvironment shifts inside chips. That's the difference between a NAM that generates a snapshot and one that can generate kinetic, mechanistic data comparable to in vivo PK/PD monitoring — a key gap regulators and drug discovery teams keep flagging.

- **Bioprinted organoids edge toward clinical translation.** A review specifically framing 3D bioprinted organoids in terms of "clinical translation" (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42502268/) is notable simply because most organoid literature stays preclinical/mechanistic. Watch this space for signs of NAMs moving from discovery-tool status toward regulatory or therapeutic application claims.

- **Disease-background organoids for tox and susceptibility modeling.** iPSC-derived liver organoids built on a MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) background showed enhanced disease susceptibility and greater acetaminophen toxicity than "healthy" organoids (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42504825/). This is directly relevant to NAM adoption in tox/safety pharma: it demonstrates that baseline patient/disease context materially changes drug toxicity readouts — an argument for building disease-state variants into standard NAM toxicology panels rather than relying on "normal" organoid lines alone.

- **Culture protocol shapes tumor organoid identity — a reproducibility flag.** Work showing that culture protocol choice controls glioblastoma tumor organoid architecture and identity when going from patient tissue to organoid (Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42503464/) is a quiet but important caution for anyone using patient-derived organoids for drug screening: protocol standardization isn't a technicality, it can determine whether the model actually reflects the tumor it's supposed to represent. Relevant to ongoing debates about NAM validation and cross-lab reproducibility.
